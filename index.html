Frequently Asked Questions

Get answers to the most common questions about buying, selling, and
investing in real estate

How do I know if I'm ready to buy a home?

You're ready to buy a home when you have stable income, a good credit score (typically 620+), a down payment saved (usually 10–20% of the home price), and you plan to stay in the area for at least 3–5 years. Our team can help assess your financial readiness and connect you with mortgage pre-approval services.

What's the best time of year to sell my home?

Spring (March–May) is traditionally the strongest selling season, as more buyers are active and homes tend to show better with warmer weather. However, local market conditions, your home's condition, and pricing strategy can matter more than the calendar.

How much should I offer on a house?

Your offer should be based on comparable sales in the neighborhood, the home's condition, how long it's been on the market, and current demand. In a competitive market you may need to offer at or above asking price; in a slower market, negotiating below is common.

What are closing costs and who pays them?

Closing costs are fees associated with finalizing a real estate transaction — typically 2–5% of the loan amount. They include lender fees, title insurance, appraisal, and prepaid taxes. Both buyers and sellers pay certain costs, though this can be negotiated.

Should I get a home inspection?

Yes — almost always. A home inspection (typically $300–$500) uncovers structural, mechanical, and safety issues before you close. It gives you leverage to negotiate repairs or price reductions, and can save you from expensive surprises down the road.

How long does it take to buy or sell a home?

Buying a home typically takes 30–60 days from accepted offer to closing, though it can vary. Selling depends on your market — homes in high-demand areas may go under contract within days, while others take weeks or months. Preparation and pricing are key.
